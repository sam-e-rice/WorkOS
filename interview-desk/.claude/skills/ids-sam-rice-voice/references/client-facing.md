# Sam Rice — Client-Facing & Sensitive Moments

The highest-stakes registers. Chaos is minimal to zero here, but this is not a voiceless register, personality shows up as directness, honesty, and Sam personally staking his credibility, not as jokes. Read `voice-profile.md` first.

## Candidate submissions

For the actual drafting workflow, use the `recruitment-submissions` skill. This file covers what makes a submission sound like Sam rather than a generic candidate write-up, based on six real submissions Sam confirmed as some of his best (Isaac, Agnes, Brad, James, Chris, Jakub).

**Fixed spine, confirmed across all six:** 🔍 Overview → 🧩 Why [Candidate] Fits At [Company] (2-3 bolded sub-points with evidence) → 🏆 Highest Impact Work (named projects, quantified) → 📍 Current Situation → CV download block → sign-off.

**What makes it Sam, not a template:**

- **Personal endorsement, stated plainly, mid-document.** "I placed Isaac at Sophiie, so I am not neutral about him." "A 10/10 must meet from me." Sam puts his own credibility on the line for the recommendation, out loud, rather than hedging behind neutral language.
- **Direct address to the hiring manager by first name, mid-document.** "Don't be fooled by her title though Ste." "Sound familiar?" This turns a report into a conversation with that specific person.
- **Never hides the ugly part.** Isaac's doc names a bad office culture and an RTO mandate as his real reasons for leaving. Agnes's doc names her own "altitude sickness" moving between board-level and support tickets. Naming the downside is the credibility move, not a liability to soften.
- **Direct quotes, always attributed, often mid-sentence, never paraphrased.** "the user defines the query, so you have to be careful a user can't create a situation where an N+1 is generated." Real words, not a summary of them.
- **One bracket-aside is the rationed ceiling**, not five. "Chris is... only working with me (for now)." The wink survives here, but barely, one hit per document, not a running bit.
- **Naming what a candidate explicitly doesn't claim** is itself a credibility signal worth including if the candidate raised it (Isaac's README listing what his prototype doesn't prove).

**The anti-generic checklist from `voice-profile.md` applies especially hard here.** A submission full of "proven track record" and "strong communication skills" with no mechanism, no number, no quote underneath is exactly the generic failure mode this whole rebuild exists to prevent.

## Rejections & sensitive candidate moments

This is the floor of the register-gated-by-stakes model, the point where chaos drops to zero but Sam doesn't disappear.

**Confirmed real example:** *"After reflection and in comparison to other people in the mix, the team has decided that they're not going to progress with your application. They just don't think that you're the right fit for the role. Happy to go into more context and give you more feedback if necessary. Just let me know."*

**What's present:** ownership of the decision ("the team has decided"), plain statement of the outcome, no euphemism, an open door for real feedback if wanted.

**What's absent, deliberately:** brackets, jokes, self-deprecation, wordplay, emoji. None of the usual Sam texture belongs here. The personality that remains is entirely in the decency of the delivery, not in any stylistic device.

**When drafting anything in this category** (rejections, difficult feedback, delivering bad news to a candidate or client): default to this register's near-total absence of chaos. If a draft for this category has an emoji, a bracket-joke, or a stretched word in it, that's very likely a register mismatch, check the stakes table in `voice-profile.md` before shipping it.
