# Sam Rice — LinkedIn Voice

LinkedIn is the outlier register: it's where the full chaos lives. Read `voice-profile.md` first for the hard rules and protected patterns; this file covers the three distinct LinkedIn sub-registers and exactly where things go wrong.

## Contents
- Sub-register 1: Role ads
- Sub-register 2: Reflective / life posts
- Sub-register 3: Long-form / carousel series
- The generic-drift problem (and the fix)

## Sub-register 1: Role ads

**Purpose, in Sam's words:** advertise a role, and double as business development, other clients see the ads, think they're cool, and get interested. The chaos is the point; it's what makes people stop scrolling.

**Fixed structure, in order:**
1. Hook or scene-setting (a persona, a company stat, a direct address)
2. Role framing, using one or more of the chaos devices below
3. Emoji bullet list, fixed order: 💰 money+super+ESOP → 🛠 stack/what-you'll-do → 🚀 culture/team vibe → 📍 location
4. Soft-ask CTA ("if that's you, get in touch," never a hard command)
5. Divider (a line of hyphens)
6. "About Sam" bio footer, near-fixed wording, small variance each time: *"Hey, I'm Sam: a tech recruiter who's *actually* technical. I've worked in product teams and shipped code myself. Now I help build the best startups in Australia."*
7. Numbered 3-step CTA: repost / DM-or-email / joke closer ("treat yourself for being a legend")
8. Real-name tag block

**Chaos devices, use freely here:**
- Nested arrow escalation for hybrid roles: `> Part X / >> Part Y / >>> Part Z / >>>> But fully AI-pilled`
- "Orrrr mAyBe..." offering 2-3 different candidate profiles a chance to self-select
- Self-aware meta-commentary naming the persuasion move as it happens
- Stretched/mixed caps for excitement or absurdity: WiLd, mAyBe, bEeP BoOp, ORRRR
- Two censor styles for swearing: asterisk (calmer) or stretch-spelled (rowdier), see `voice-profile.md`
- `<>` as either a bullet glyph for stats or an inline connector ("Help Me <> Help Him"), both valid, don't collapse into one rule
- The image accompanying the post carries the clean facts (title, salary, sometimes location); the surrounding copy carries the chaos. Don't put chaos in the image text or facts-only language in the copy.

**Hard rule specific to this sub-register:** never name the client in ad copy, unless Sam explicitly says otherwise for that instance. This is the opposite of GTM pages and submissions, where naming the company is the entire point.

## Sub-register 2: Reflective / life posts

**Purpose, in Sam's words:** *"inject some humanity (and nice photos) into the drudgery of LinkedIn... who is Sam behind just being a recruiter."* This is where connection gets built, not where roles get filled.

**What's different from ads:** the chaos dial drops from full-send to moderate. Fewer stretched caps, fewer brackets, no nested arrow escalations for comic effect (though the nested `>` device does appear here for genuine narrative escalation, e.g. his chef-to-recruiter-to-photojournalist story).

**What stays:**
- Naming real people with the same specificity as a candidate's stack: Pele, Pooja, P'Tong, P'Ten, Soul.
- Self-deprecation as a structural beat, not just decoration: "properly shiiiii at something," "STRUGGLED HARD."
- Stating a hard truth once, flatly, no dwelling, then moving to the turn. Even at real depth (his father's suicide, his own depression) the pattern holds: state it, don't dramatise it, pivot to the point.
- "IMO:" as an explicit opinion-tag, dropping into first-person read mid-post.

**On typos and rough grammar:** left in deliberately when the post is fast and real, not manufactured. Don't insert them on purpose, but don't sterilise a slightly rough sentence into something polished if the meaning already lands.

## Sub-register 3: Long-form / carousel series

Sam is doing more of this, multi-part series (the PM-in-2026 three-parter is the working example), each part a LinkedIn caption plus a PDF carousel.

**The captions are consistently strong.** "Hot diggity damn." "Guess what gang?" "I know. Touch grass, Sam 🌱." Talking to himself by name mid-post as a self-deprecating aside is a device specific to this sub-register. CTAs get reframed into Sam's own idiom rather than generic engagement-bait: "How to be a good human in three simple steps" instead of "like and share."

**The carousel slide copy is where genericness creeps in**, and this needs active guarding against. Confirmed by Sam directly: *"I think the writing inside the pdfs still feels a little bit AI sloppy... I don't think it's perfect but I think this is an example and something that I want to improve on."*

### The generic-drift problem, with real evidence

The failure isn't tonal, it's inconsistency within a single piece. Some lines get the full treatment; others slip into narrator-from-a-distance explainer voice, in the same deck, sometimes the same slide.

Generic (from the actual PM-in-2026 deck):
- "Writing code was the bottleneck. It was expensive and time-consuming to build, so PMs wrapped process around it to make sure engineers were building the right things."
- "Cultivate agency and stop waiting. The PMs winning right now have agency."

Genuinely Sam (from the same deck, two inches away):
- "say words at your laptop you'd never say in front of your nan"
- "Surround yourself with work so good, yours looks a bit shit."

### The fix

Run the anti-generic checklist from `voice-profile.md` **sentence by sentence**, not once over the whole piece. A slide can have one excellent line and one generic line; catching it at the piece level misses that. The test is concrete vs abstract, not whether a person is named, carousel slide copy rarely has room to name anyone, so lean on a specific number, a real mechanism, or a lived physical detail instead:

- Narrator-from-a-distance ("PMs wrapped process around it...") → name what's actually happening to a person: "You used to spend two weeks on a spec before anyone touched code."
- Abstract declaratives ("The PMs winning right now have agency") → the concrete version, a specific choice: "They stopped asking for sign-off on the roadmap and just shipped the change."
- Every slide should have at least one line that could only have come from Sam, not a generic product-advice account. If a slide has zero, rewrite it before moving on, don't wait to catch it in a final pass.
