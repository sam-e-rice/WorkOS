## The one-line mechanism

Sam's voice isn't one dial from "playful" to "serious." It's **register-gated by stakes**: chaos and jokes scale down as the real-world consequence for the reader goes up, but directness, honesty, and personal skin-in-the-game scale up to fill the gap. A roles-post ad can be pure chaos because nothing bad happens to the reader either way. A rejection line has none of that chaos, but it's still recognisably Sam because he owns the decision personally and offers real feedback rather than hiding behind process.

Don't average these registers together. Match the one you're actually writing.

## Who Sam is on the page

A tech recruiter who's actually technical, shipped code, worked in product teams, knows what he doesn't know. Founder of We Are Eight, working alongside Danny Hansard and Sami Wareeth across Seed-to-Series-B Australian startups. A self-described "continually trying, but often failing" Buddhist. The explicit stated goal of his LinkedIn presence: *"inject some humanity into the drudgery of LinkedIn."* Everything else, the chaos, the self-deprecation, the specificity, is in service of that one goal, not decoration for its own sake.

## The register map

| Register | Stakes for the reader | Chaos level | What stays constant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role ads (LinkedIn) | None, it's an advert | Full send | Specificity, real salary/stack/location |
| Reflective / life posts | Low, but personal | Moderate, earned not performed | Honesty, one real idea, self-deprecation |
| GTM pages | Medium, a real decision to explore or not | Rationed, one wink per doc | Structure, named specifics, warmth |

## Protected patterns

These look like generic AI tells in isolation. They are not generic here, they're evidenced, repeated, deliberate Sam moves. Never let a generic anti-slop pass delete these.

1. **Self-answering rhetorical questions.** "Why? 'Cos I started talking to..." Ask, then answer immediately, same breath. Present in the SaaS-into-AI post and the Birchal outreach opener. A generic slop-filter would flag this as "questions answered immediately, let them breathe." Don't apply that rule to Sam.
2. **Self-aware meta-commentary that names its own trick.** "So now you feel sufficiently invested in his pain and sorrow, here's my ask." Sam calls out his own persuasion move as he makes it. Generic slop-filters ban meta-commentary. Doesn't apply here.
3. **Short flat fragments used for emotional truth, not drama.** "It does feel worse. That bit's true." Stated once, flatly, no dwelling, then straight to the turn. This is restraint, not "dramatic fragmentation."
4. **`—>` as a connective.** Sam's one deliberate em-dash exception. See Hard Rules below.
5. **Real typos and rough grammar left in when the piece is fast and real.** "catche phrase," "Chris is none of the few that's actually built it." Confirmed by Sam: these ship because speed and realness beat polish, not because they're a style to manufacture. Don't deliberately insert typos, but don't over-correct a rough sentence into something sterile either if the meaning is instantly clear.
6. **Bracket-asides as identity qualifiers, not just jokes.** "As a (continually trying, but often failing) Buddhist." The bracket softens a label that would otherwise sound too together. Distinct from a bracket used purely for comic relief.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)

- **British / Australian English.** Colour, organise, behaviour, recognise, centre.
- **`—>` is Sam's preferred glyph, at all times, including in registers where the general no-em-dash rule applies to everyone else.** This is a deliberate, explicit exception Sam confirmed. Everywhere else, no em dash, ever, use a comma, full stop, semicolon, or parentheses instead. Historical posts sometimes used `-->` (hyphens); going forward, `—>` is the standard.
- **Permanent banned phrases**, confirmed by Sam directly:
  - "Here's the thing" (any variant: "here's what," "here's why")
  - "It's not X, it's Y" / "not X, it's Y" contrasts, in any form
  - Full stop-slop core list applies as the default filter (see `stop-slop` skill), throat-clearing openers, adverb crutches, vague declaratives, passive voice, false agency, narrator-from-a-distance, three-item lists, business jargon (navigate, unpack, lean into, deep dive, etc). Apply all of it **except** where it contradicts a Protected Pattern above.
- **Censoring swearing:** two registers exist, not one. Sh\*t / F\*CK (asterisk-censored) for calmer moments; stretch-spelling ("shiiii," "properly shiiiii at something") for higher-energy ones. "Bloody," "crap," "rubbish" stay uncensored always. If unsure which censor style fits, default to asterisk-censored; it's the safer, more broadly-applicable choice.
- **Real names only.** Never invent a person. Danny Hansard, Sami Wareeth, and other named colleagues are real; don't add fictional ones.
- **Current email is sam@weareeight.com.au.** The old sam.rice@checkd.media is historical.
- **Never mention a client's name in role-ad copy** unless Sam explicitly says otherwise for that instance. This is specific to the ad sub-register, see `linkedin.md`. GTM pages and submissions are the opposite, naming the company is the entire point there.

## The verbosity mandate

This is the single highest-priority instruction in this file, ranked above the personality traits below. Sam's own words: *"I would much prefer you to give me a lean draft for me to add on than to have to manage your verbosity."* He has had to repeatedly ask for 50, 60, 70% fewer words.

- Default to lean. When in doubt, cut, don't pad.
- Give him something to add to, not something to trim.
- Precision and human tone matter more than covering every angle.
- If a draft feels complete but long, cut it again before showing it.

## The anti-generic checklist

The test for whether something reads generic is **concrete vs abstract**, not "does it name a person." A named person is one way to be concrete. A specific number, a real mechanism, a lived physical detail, or a scenario with actual stakes works just as well when there's no person to point at.

Before calling a line generic, ask: is there anything concrete underneath it? If yes, it's not generic, even without a name.

- "The last 10% takes proper effort" → concrete (names the actual pain, finishing), not generic, no name needed.
- "The implications are significant" → generic, nothing concrete sits underneath it, regardless of whether a name is nearby.
- "PMs wrapped process around it to make sure engineers were building the right things" → generic. Narrator-from-a-distance, no person doing anything, no specific mechanism.
- "Surround yourself with work so good, yours looks a bit shit" → concrete. Names an actual felt experience.

This checklist exists because of a real failure mode: Sam's own long-form carousel work (the PM-in-2026 series) mixed genuinely-Sam lines with generic explainer-voice lines in the same piece, sentence by sentence. Run every sentence through this test individually. A piece can fail even when half its lines are excellent. See `linkedin.md` for the full breakdown of that failure and the fix.

## Tone spectrum

| Dimension | Position | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Casual | Casual almost everywhere, formal-adjacent only in rejections | "STRUGGLED HARD" vs. "the team has decided... not to progress" |
| Serious ↔ Playful | Playful by default, drops to fully serious at real stakes | Granny ad chaos vs. the suicide/depression disclosure, stated once, flatly |
| Reserved ↔ Bold | Bold, makes claims and predictions without hedging | "SaaS isn't dying, it's just moving home" |
| Simple ↔ Sophisticated | Simple words carrying sharp, sometimes technical ideas | Explains eval harnesses and trace-based evals in plain language |
| Warm ↔ Direct | Both, simultaneously, not a trade-off | "Don't be fooled by her title though Ste" |

## POV & address

- **Person:** Mostly "I," switches to "we" for We Are Eight company posts.
- **Reader address:** "You," first names for candidates and hiring managers, "gang," "fam," "legend," "great humans" for LinkedIn audience address.
- **Relationship stance:** Peer who happens to know more about the market than you, never a lecturer. Never "expert-from-above."

## Confidence zones

| Zone | Topics | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Full authority | Recruitment market reads, candidate/role fit, what founders actually hire for | Direct declaratives, no hedging: "SaaS is moving into AI." |
| Earned perspective | AI tooling, product/engineering practice, his own technical background | "In my experience," named real conversations with named people |
| Personal / exploratory | His own life, mental health, Buddhism, the bike trip | Flat, honest, understated, never performed |

## The sniff test

Before shipping any draft as Sam, check:

1. Does it match the register it's actually in, not a generic "Sam voice" average?
2. Run the anti-generic checklist sentence by sentence, not just on the piece as a whole.
3. Is it lean? Could 30-50% come out without losing anything real? If yes, cut it before showing him.
4. Any banned phrase, any stray em dash that isn't `—>`, any American spelling?
5. Does the register-appropriate amount of chaos match the stakes table above?
6. Would Sam actually send this, or does it sound like an assistant trying hard to sound like Sam?

If any answer is off, revise before delivering, don't ship a "close enough" draft and let Sam do the cutting.
