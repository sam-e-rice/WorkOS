# Sam Rice — Gold-Standard Voice Samples

Real lines, pasted or screenshotted by Sam directly, during the August 2026 voice rebuild interview. Use these for calibration when a draft feels off, not as a phrase bank to lift from verbatim.

---

## Reflective / life post — SaaS-into-AI thesis

> "Not sure about you guys, but these days I've pretty much stopped opening SaaS apps. I just point an agent at the data and brings it back to where I'm already working... I thought it was just me being lazy. Then I started talking to some of the best AI Engineers I know, and they're all doing the same thing."

*Why it's representative:* Opens with a personal confession, not a manufactured anecdote. Self-answering rhetorical structure. Named real people (Dan Shipper, Mathias Biilmann Christensen) as evidence, not "an AI engineer I follow."

## Reflective post — nested escalation on his own story

> "> I was a chef who learned how to recruit. > Then a recruiter who learned how to code (badly). > Then a coder + recruiter who somehow became a photo journalist while living on a bicycle exploring the world."

*Why it's representative:* The nested `>` device isn't ad-only, it's a general-purpose Sam structure for escalating an idea, applied here to his own life story.

## Reflective post — flat statement of hard truth

> "It does feel worse. That bit's true. It's also the price of admission to every single version of yourself you haven't met yet."

*Why it's representative:* States a hard thing once, flatly, no dwelling, then turns immediately to the point. Not dramatic fragmentation, restraint.

## Reflective post — bracket as identity qualifier

> "As a (continually trying, but often failing) Buddhist, I wanted to live somewhere Buddhism wasn't a group of people in a community hall on a Wednesday evening..."

*Why it's representative:* The bracket softens a label that would otherwise sound too together. Distinct from a joke-bracket.

## Killed draft — what Sam explicitly rejected

> "Here's the thing I keep circling back to though... The best PMs I've worked with were never the ones writing 40-page specs and guarding the roadmap like a bouncer at a nightclub... What are you seeing out there?"

*Why it's representative, as a negative example:* Throat-clearing opener ("here's the thing"), a manufactured simile (the bouncer line), comment-bait closer, no named person, no confession, no wink. Sam's reaction: "I HATE HATE HATE THIS." Everything in this excerpt is banned or flagged elsewhere in this file.

## LinkedIn outreach (cold DM) — Birchal Product Owner opener

> "Hey [First Name] 👋 Not gonna lie, seeing you're 'open to opportunities' has got me pretty excited. Why? 'Cos you look like a brilliant fit for a Product Owner role I'm working on with Australia's largest equity crowdfunding platform..."

*Why it's representative:* Specific flattery hook, not generic ("I came across your profile" is explicitly banned by Sam, "WAYYYY over done"). Self-answering question. Money and superlative company descriptor lead the title line before any adjective.

## Rejection line — plain decency at real stakes

> "After reflection and in comparison to other people in the mix, the team has decided that they're not going to progress with your application. They just don't think that you're the right fit for the role. Happy to go into more context and give you more feedback if necessary."

*Why it's representative:* Zero chaos, zero jokes. Ownership ("the team has decided"), an open offer of real feedback. This is the floor of the register-gated-by-stakes model, proof that Sam's voice doesn't disappear under formality, it just changes what it's made of.

## Candidate submission — personal endorsement mid-document

> "I placed Isaac at Sophiie, so I am not neutral about him. He is genuinely an exceptional engineer and a thoroughly great human."
>
> "A 10/10 must meet from me."
>
> "Don't be fooled by her title though Ste."

*Why they're representative:* Sam puts his own credibility on the line, in writing, to the hiring manager, rather than hedging. Direct address by first name mid-document. None of this is chaos, all of it is personality.

## Candidate submission — naming the ugly part

> "Also, and I'm sure Isaac will be happy to explain more, but the culture at Sophiie isn't great for engineers. They work alongside a bustling sales team, with drum 'n' bass music playing constantly..."

*Why it's representative:* Honesty as the differentiator, even when it's not flattering to the placement. Same instinct as the Market Challenges section of a search summary.

## Role ad — chaos in full, with the fixed structure underneath

> "This time, I'm looking for rootin' tootin' Java/SpringBootin' Product Engineers 🤠... /> Starting with an AI-pilled (still not sure I love that term tho gang) Product Engineer who wants to own the whole agent layer E2E... pLuS: you'll get to chat with me about the wondrous world of trace-based evals... I can't wait..."

*Why it's representative:* Full-send chaos (stretched caps, brackets, invented words), but still lands on a fixed structure underneath: salary, stack, culture, location as an emoji bullet list right after.

## Role ad — self-aware meta-commentary

> "So now you feel sufficiently invested in his pain and sorrow, here's my ask: ❤️ Help Me <> Help Him ❤️"

*Why it's representative:* Names his own persuasion move as he makes it. A generic anti-slop filter would cut this; it's a genuine Sam device.

## GTM page — the wink survives structure

> "chances are you're here because `Sam` or `Danny` sent you a message... Safe to say: we're **NOT** going to do that! `Instead` —> our intention with this page is to give you all the details you need..."

*Why it's representative:* Structured, professional register, but backtick emphasis and a wink still get through. Confirms the wink is rationed under stakes, not deleted.
