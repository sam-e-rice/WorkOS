## Poetry

Melanie Walsh's study of 5,700 ChatGPT poems vs. 3,700 human poems (arXiv 2410.15299): GPT poetry is "much more constrained and uniform."

- Unrequested rhyme, quatrains, and iambic meter as defaults
- First-person-plural overrepresentation ("we, us, our" — curiously dominant vs. human poems)
- Signature vocabulary: *heart, embrace, echo(es), whisper(s)*
- Mood-word accumulation: *ache, hollow, tether, linger, fragile, fractured, ember, bloom, cradle, ruin, veil, threadbare*
- Aphorism density: every line a pull-quote; epigrams stacked with no connective tissue
- Line-level negate-and-redefine: "Not an ending / but a beginning"; "I am not water, / I am the thirst"
- Abstraction drift: pivoting to "the soul," "the silence," "the weight of what remains" instead of staying with a physical image
- No grain: no proper nouns, months, brand names, dialect
- Superficial form fidelity: a 39-line "sestina" without the end-word pattern holding
- The bodiless lyric "I": first person unattached to situation, history, or place

## Fiction

Neil Clarke (Clarkesworld) closed submissions Feb 2023 when AI hit ~500 of 1,200 weekly stories: "bad in ways that no human has been bad before. They are boring. They are flat."

- Dialogue flattening: one shared idiolect; no verbal tics, dialect, or evasion patterns
- Parenthetical stage directions (esp. GPT-3.5): "Bob: (defensive) Why should I?"
- As-you-know-Bob exposition: mysteries resolved by explanatory speech, not action
- POV lock: no drift between free indirect style, interior monologue, scene
- Pacing flatness: constant scene rate; no elision
- Mood-saturated weather: "The rain pattered softly against the window, mirroring her unspoken grief."
- Resolution bias: endings tie up; ambiguity isn't trusted
- Right-ballpark-wrong-physics metaphors: "a river of logic that bloomed in the garden of her mind"
- Expert readers detect via "clichés, purple prose, too much exposition, lack of subtext, mixed metaphors"; fine-tuning suppresses these and drops detection accuracy (Dhillon et al., arXiv 2510.13939)

## Student writing

Waltzer et al. (2023): 69 teachers + 140 students judged paired essays — teacher accuracy 70%, students 62%; confidence uncorrelated with accuracy. Well-written student essays were hardest to distinguish; teachers often flagged the *better* essays as AI.

What teachers actually notice:
- Sudden-polish jumps above the student's demonstrated baseline
- Absence of personal reflection where the prompt invited it
- Vocabulary mismatch ("ubiquitous," "paradigm," "juxtapose" from a basic-level writer)
- Missing or hallucinated citations
- Rubric-language echoing
- One-paste Google Docs version history
- Inability to discuss the essay aloud

## Academic writing

- Kobak et al.: ≥13.5% of 2024 PubMed abstracts (up to 40% in MDPI/Frontiers subcorpora) show LLM fingerprints — a vocabulary shift exceeding COVID's effect.
- Overgeneralized abstracts emphasizing "significance" without naming findings
- Section summaries reproducing the paper's own claims verbatim
- Fewer engagement markers (questions, asides, reader address) than human essays (Jiang & Hyland 2025)
- Real 2023 PubMed example: "By **meticulously delving into the intricate web** connecting […], this **comprehensive** chapter **takes a deep dive into** their involvement as **significant** risk factors…"

## Journalism

Documented scandals: CNET's 77 AI finance articles (corrections on more than half), Sports Illustrated's fake-bylined reviews, Gannett/G-O Media/BuzzFeed incidents.

- Product round-ups with generic praise ("durable construction," "excellent value," "users rave")
- Identical paragraph structures across unrelated articles
- Quotes from unidentified "experts" — no names, institutions, contacts
- Boilerplate bios of fictional reporters; datelines mismatched with events

## Email & business writing

(Gmelius catalog; Bynder 2024: 55% of U.S. consumers correctly identify AI marketing content.)

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- Three-sentence paragraphs of 30–50 words each
- Unedited placeholders ("Hi {client_name}")
- "Please let me know if there's anything else I can help with!" (regardless of direction)
- Paragraph tricolons ("No fluff. No filler. No stress.")
- Loss of prior-thread context
- Absence of colons/semicolons (replaced by commas or em dashes)
